# Spoken language-ID teacher candidates (as of 2026-09-25)

Research date: **2026-09-25**. This is a snapshot, not a claim about releases after that date. Hugging Face model metadata and linked primary sources were checked on the research date. “Unverified” means I found no primary-source measurement for that property; resampling support is not evidence of robustness to 8 kHz/telephone-band speech.

## Bottom line for this project

1. **Benchmark AmberNet/TitaNet-LID first.** The official 29M-parameter NVIDIA model is almost as small as the current 21.3M SpeechBrain ECAPA teacher, predicts the same 107 labels, and has the best directly comparable short-clip result: **7.5% error on 0–5 s clips versus 11.9% for SpeechBrain ECAPA**. The convenient HF port, [`surogate/ambernet-langid`](https://huggingface.co/surogate/ambernet-langid), is community-maintained, so its numerical parity with the official NGC checkpoint must be checked before accepting it.
2. **Keep SpeechBrain ECAPA as the reproducible baseline.** It is small, Apache-2.0, already integrated, and covers all seven project labels (`en hi mr bn ta te gu`). Its principal weaknesses are worse published short-clip accuracy than AmberNet, YouTube-domain bias, and no published 8 kHz result.
3. **Do not move the main CPU pipeline to MMS, GeoLID, Seamless, Qwen, or Whisper-large.** Their roughly 0.8–2.3B parameter footprints are appropriate for offline label adjudication or cached experiments, not repeated sliding-window inference on CPU. This is a footprint-based engineering inference; comparable x86 CPU benchmarks are not published.
4. **The most relevant Indic-specific released family is Voxlect.** It covers 22 scheduled Indian languages plus Indian English and publishes a duration split, but its best variant is a 974M MMS model under a non-commercial license. The 90.4M Whisper-small encoder variant is the only remotely CPU-plausible one, yet it scores only 75.0% overall and its repository code needs a CPU-device fix.
5. **No candidate has verified Hindi–English short-clip accuracy at 8 kHz.** MMS and GeoLID have useful BABEL telephone-domain results, but those are long utterances, not a Hindi–English 8 kHz test. A local band-limit/codec benchmark is therefore mandatory.

Scores below are not interchangeable: label inventories, datasets, macro versus micro averaging, and clip duration differ. A high FLEURS score does not establish code-switch performance or frame-level behavior.

## Detailed observations

### 1. SpeechBrain ECAPA versus AmberNet is the fair comparison

Both models use VoxLingua107, cover exactly the project's seven labels, are utterance classifiers, and are small enough for CPU target generation. The [TitaNet-LID paper, v2 dated 2023-08-10](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.15781) evaluates both on the same official clips and duration bins. Its table reports:

| Model | Parameters | 0–5 s error | 5–20 s error | Overall error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SpeechBrain ECAPA checkpoint | 21M | 11.9% | 6.7% | 7.5% |
| TitaNet-LID 3x5x1024 | 28.9M | **7.5%** | **5.2%** | **5.6%** |

That is a 4.4-point absolute short-bin improvement under this paper's evaluation. The [SpeechBrain model card (accessed 2026-09-25)](https://huggingface.co/speechbrain/lang-id-voxlingua107-ecapa) separately reports 6.7% overall, while the [official NVIDIA NGC card, updated 2023-04-04](https://catalog.ngc.nvidia.com/orgs/nvidia/nemo/models/langid_ambernet) reports 5.22% for AmberNet. Because those card totals do not exactly match the paper's re-evaluation, use the paper table for head-to-head comparison and reproduce locally before drawing conclusions.

The official NVIDIA artifact is NeMo/NGC rather than Hugging Face. The 2026 community conversion provides plain PyTorch and ONNX and claims a strong CPU RTF, but this conversion is not an NVIDIA release. Required gate: compare its logits/top-1 against the official `.nemo` checkpoint on identical 16 kHz clips.

### 2. MMS and GeoLID are strong offline teachers, not deployment-shaped teachers

The [MMS paper, JMLR 2024](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume25/23-1318/23-1318.pdf) supplies broad, directly usable LID heads. `mms-lid-126` is the more sensible of its two heads for this project: it is marginally better than the 4,017-way head on FLEURS and BABEL, while still covering all seven selected labels. The 4,017-way checkpoint is relevant only if the desired student inventory expands to low-resource Indian languages.

[GeoLID, posted 2025-08-23](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17148), improves cross-domain aggregate results by adding ECAPA and geolocation-aware supervision on top of MMS features. Its BABEL 95.4% is the strongest telephone-domain figure found in this sweep. However, the checkpoint is still about 3.91 GB, the BABEL utterances are long, and the Hub/upstream license metadata conflicts. It is better suited to one-time cached target generation than iterative CPU experiments.

Neither model exposes causal frame-level language decisions. A sliding window can produce pseudo-frame targets, but that changes the measurement protocol and repeats an expensive encoder pass.

### 3. Whisper, Qwen, and Seamless are ASR systems with LID side effects

Whisper's [`detect_language`](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/main/whisper/decoding.py) is convenient and returns probabilities over language tokens, but it runs an encoder over a padded/trimmed audio segment and yields one decision. The [Whisper paper, dated 2022-12](https://cdn.openai.com/papers/whisper.pdf) reports broad FLEURS LID, not the short Hindi–English/Indic condition here.

Qwen3-ASR is newer and stronger on its own 30-language LID protocol. Its [official HF card (accessed 2026-09-25)](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-0.6B-hf) shows that auto-detection is a generated prefix (`language English<asr_text>...`), not a ready probability vector. More importantly, it covers only Hindi and English from the seven target labels.

SeamlessM4T performs source-language-aware multilingual ASR/translation, but neither its [official HF card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/seamless-m4t-v2-large) nor public generation API documents a calibrated standalone LID head. [`facebook/w2v-bert-2.0`](https://huggingface.co/facebook/w2v-bert-2.0) does provide useful frame-indexed representations, but it has no LID classifier. Counting either as a drop-in teacher would be misleading.

### 4. Indic-specific releases need stronger release hygiene

The [Voxlect paper, posted 2025-08-03](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01691) is the most relevant released Indic benchmark because it tests all 23 output classes and reports a duration split. Its result also warns against assuming a larger generic foundation model solves short Indic LID: even the best MMS variant falls to .690 macro-F1 at ≤6 s. The repository's current CPU incompatibility and non-commercial terms prevent it from being a clean replacement for the Apache-licensed baseline.

The [Vaani 42-language comparison, posted 2026-06-08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09317) is newer and covers many more varieties, but I could not locate the trained LID heads on the Hub. The underlying [SraVaani FastConformer paper, posted 2026-08-08](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08235) and base checkpoint do not make the study's classifier a released teacher. This item is therefore explicitly **unverified/unavailable**, not a recommendation.

One smaller community checkpoint, [`panchajanya-ai/lid_Indic_Vaani`](https://huggingface.co/panchajanya-ai/lid_Indic_Vaani), uses an approximately 21M ECAPA classifier for English, Hindi, Marathi, Tamil and Telugu under Apache-2.0. It misses Bengali and Gujarati and publishes no dataset provenance, accuracy, duration, 8 kHz, or CPU benchmark, so it is not shortlist-quality.

Three March 2026 community uploads—[`ansc00053/indic-language-identification`](https://huggingface.co/ansc00053/indic-language-identification), [`radioduran/indic-language-identification`](https://huggingface.co/radioduran/indic-language-identification), and [`PremKumar12/indic-language-identification`](https://huggingface.co/PremKumar12/indic-language-identification)—were also screened out. They have 22 Indian-language heads but omit English, and their autogenerated cards provide no license, training data, or evaluation. Treat all quality claims for them as **unverified**.

## Recommended local evaluation protocol

Run SpeechBrain ECAPA and the AmberNet HF conversion on exactly the same clips, without changing downstream smoothing:

1. Use the project's seven classes twice: (a) normal 107-way argmax and (b) renormalize probabilities over `en hi mr bn ta te gu`. Record both, because removing unrelated classes may improve confidence while hiding out-of-set errors.
2. For every labeled clip, score full utterance and 0.5, 1, 2, 3 and 5 s prefixes. Report top-1, macro-F1, NLL/Brier score and confidence. Do not call a 3 s result “frame-level.”
3. Create paired channel conditions: native 16 kHz; low-pass to 3.4 kHz then resample to 8 kHz and back to 16 kHz; G.711 μ-law/A-law if available; and additive noise. The model still receives 16 kHz tensors, but the information content matches telephone bandwidth.
4. Measure single-thread and four-thread CPU RTF, peak RSS, warm/cold latency, and batch-size-one latency on the target machine. Community benchmark numbers are not substitutes.
5. Verify the HF AmberNet port against official NeMo on at least 30 clips: same top-1 on all but documented floating-point ties and maximum absolute logit/probability deviation below a declared tolerance.

For a 30-clip labeled set, one extra correct prediction is 3.33 percentage points. State counts and Wilson intervals rather than interpreting a one-clip change as a stable gain.
